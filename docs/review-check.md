## What the reviewer knew, and the marked drafts (prereg text, as locked)

**reviewer_knew.** Before reviewing, the reviewer had read, in Claude Code's Block 0 report and in DECISIONS.md (2026-11-05), the IDs of the four households with show-class runs, that every show-class problem is a dropped R001 beside a wrong W-2 count, that no show-class run is on A-opus, that no carried fact is wrong, and that the draw took 6 drafts from each of the four A configs. In a progress note Claude Code posted while building, he had seen that the agent's own notes or closing message mention 14 of the 17 problems, including 6 of the 9 hidden in drafts. He had read the PRD's section 5 worked example, written before the draw, which gave the review-draft ID d07 an invented household, run, class and edit; it says nothing about the real d07, and the example now uses d00. An uncommitted version of the 2026-11-05 DECISIONS.md entry also gave one example of how a hidden problem's note can read and named each demo draft's source run, config included; both were cut before the first commit, and he may have read them. He said on 2026-11-06 that before the re-export that day collapsed a doubled label in some agent notes, he had opened none of the files in data/drafts or preparer/app/demo_drafts, eval/export_drafts.py or docs/review-demo.html, so the doubled labels did not reach him. Between the re-export and the first commit, eval/export_drafts.py held a docstring that tied the collapsed label to config, and its masker docstring and tests/test_preparer_drafts.py paired a review draft's label or ID, as examples, with a real household number or with an invented household, config and run; all were cut before the first commit. Asked about that window, he said he opened one or both of those files, so he is taken to have read every version of both from the re-export to the first commit, as rebuilt in data/truth/review_window.json. Tool results in the Claude Code session where the marked drafts were chosen carried the analysts' counts, and one on 2026-11-06, during Block 4, printed how many drafts were marked; he was asked not to open any of them. The report gives, each on its own line, his actions summed over the drafts with a dropped:R001 flag (W-2 count raised, W-2 employers asked about, neither) and his actions on example_draft.

**reviewer_rule.** Until all 24 are approved, the reviewer opens none of the folders in reviewer_opens_none_of, runs none of the programs in reviewer_runs_none_of, and does not rebuild what those folders hold by hand from their code. The offline test suite, which rebuilds them only in temporary folders and prints no class, does not count.

**inferable_rule.** Committed text, read with what reviewer_knew lists, the design above (4, 8 and 12 drafts by class, one per household, 6 per config) and each draft's own screen, could let a reader single out a draft's class. Committed text means the files git tracks at the lock commit outside data/truth, data/profiles, eval/runs and ledger. A draft is marked when that reading gives its class clearly better than the flags alone do (six drafts carry dropped:R001). Elimination counts only when every step is itself reading. Running or reverse-engineering code to rebuild what those four folders hold does not count, because reviewer_rule rules it out. Before the lock, two subagents working from the answer key independently marked the drafts, a third settled their disagreement, and the marking was checked again against everything this feature commits. The marked IDs are committed only as inferable_sha256, the SHA-256 of the salt, a colon, and the sorted IDs joined by commas; the salt and the IDs are in data/truth/review_inferable.json, gitignored until scoring. If anything else is committed between the marking and the lock, two analysts recheck the marks against the lock commit's tree, the salt is drawn again, and inferable_sha256 is updated in the lock commit; the salt is drawn again every time, so a new hash says nothing about whether the marks changed. The report gives the shows catch rate without the marked drafts beside the full rate. Marked: d02, d03, d04, d09, d11, d14, d17, d18, d19, d20, d23. Salt: `d9904eb0f5ca739bf240eb0b3fcb66c2`, which with the marked IDs gives the prereg's `inferable_sha256` (`33853ed25179c3033c8f29ea73ad65901c55e6fa9fe8dcbd7464214c46db65ce`).

## Limits

- One reviewer, who also built the project, and 24 drafts, so every interval is wide.
- 4 drafts show their problem, because only that many households in the test run have one.
- The reviewer knew the shape of the shown problems before reviewing (above).
- Time on screen adds up, for each opening that saved or approved, the time from the server's opening, made once the screen had loaded, to that opening's last save or approval. The server stamps whole seconds.
